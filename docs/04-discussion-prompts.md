# 4. Link the discussion to the activity

Keep your current discussion prompt. Add one line that asks for the artifact and makes it the evidence for the post.

## The line to add

> Attach the artifact you built in this module's activity (a screenshot, photo or pasted table) and use it as evidence for your answer.

## Before and after

**Before (MET AD 599, Discussion 2):**

> In Python, conditional statements and loops are essential for program control. Think of a business scenario (e.g., credit scoring, dynamic pricing, or fraud detection) where if/else logic or loops could be used to automate decision-making. Describe how the logic would work and why Python is a suitable tool.

**After:**

> ...the same prompt...
> Attach the card you built in the /w02/ activity and use it as evidence: your question is the scenario, your inputs and outputs show how the logic works, and your "Excel or Python?" verdict shows why Python is suitable.

## A post built this way has four parts

1. **My artifact:** a screenshot, photo or pasted table of what I built.
2. **What it shows:** one finding, stated with something specific from my own work.
3. **Connection:** where this shows up in practice, with a specific case.
4. **Reference:** if your course requires one.

## Replies compare artifacts

Because every student worked from a different input, replies have something real to compare: "Your verdict was different from mine. Here is why I think that happened."

## Rubric

Especially for online sections, the rubric must account for the activity, or students will skip it. One criterion is enough:

| Criterion | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Activity evidence | The artifact is attached and the post uses it to answer the prompt | The artifact is missing, or attached but never used |
